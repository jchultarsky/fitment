# fitment M7: the fit verifier

As of 2026-10-22. Status: **revised after its soundness and feasibility reviews**, every finding and open question decided by the coordinator (section 15 lists what changed and why; section 13 the two points the re-run leaves for the user). Nothing here is implemented; section 11 is the order of work. The rest of the document assumes every default in section 2.

M7 delivers the fit verifier. A candidate whose interface passes is placed in the assembly at every occurrence of the part it replaces, and its body is checked against every other solid and against its own other copies by Open CASCADE, run in a separate helper process (D1). Only a verified fit makes a `Match`; a body that interferes makes a `Reject`; everything else leaves an `InterfaceMatch` with the reason the fit is not verified. PLAN's exit for M7: "On the corpus, colliding candidates end as `Reject`, or as `InterfaceMatch` with fit not verified, never as `Match`; the original part passes its own fit check." This document is that design.

**How it was made.** One designer, on `m7/design` (cut from `m6/release`, `51d671d`: fitment 0.3.0, `EXTRACTOR_VERSION` 6, `SOCKET_RULES_VERSION` 7, `SIGNATURE_VERSION` 2, `MATCH_RULES_VERSION` 1), read PLAN, ARCHITECTURE, the D1 research, M4's verdicts, fit status, MF rules, corpus and gate, M3's V1, M5's QR6, QR13 and ST11, M6's OP9, SN11, SV8, JS1 to JS8 and its image, the code's `FitVerifier` seam (`crates/fitment-core/src/fit.rs`, `matching/matcher.rs`, `fitment-server/src/matching.rs`) and the two Open CASCADE tools (`tools/gen-corpus.py`, `tools/verify-occt.py`). Every claim that rests on Open CASCADE's behaviour was checked by a probe on cadquery-ocp-novtk 8.0.1.1.0 (the pin of `tools/verify-occt.py`, and so of the helper), on an Apple M5 Max under macOS (Darwin 27), with the corpus regenerated at this commit (`tools/gen-corpus.py corpus/out`: 463 files, 129 match cases) and the release `fitment` built from it. The most important probe is a working prototype of the whole check (P2), run over the corpus and the fixtures, and re-run after the reviews with their rules.

## 1. Summary

- **What is checked.** At each occurrence k of the target part, the candidate placed by its interface's own placement, T_k · A, never re-aligned (FT2), against every solid of the assembly but the selected occurrences, and against its own copies at every other occurrence of every socket (FT3, M3's V1). Two tests per pair: **interference**, the Common of the two bodies less the zones the socket declares, must have no volume (FT4); and **proximity**, the body less its zones must stay farther than a threshold from the candidate (FT5), because Open CASCADE absorbs an overlap of 1e-7 mm and less (P1). Each pair has its own tolerances (FT7): with t the policy's linear tolerance and u_pair the larger stated uncertainty of the two bodies, zones are ε_pair = t − u_pair deep, the proximity threshold is t + u_pair, and ε_pair ≤ 0 is `not_verified` (`uncertainty_above_tolerance`). As first drafted, an uncertain neighbour deepened its own zones (P7: a 0.04 mm intrusion verified at 0.058 mm); the soundness review's C1 fixed that.
- **Zones are the assembly's declared contacts,** derived by fitment-core from the socket's requirements, never by the helper: each seat contact's neighbour face swept ε_pair either side of its plane, each interference, line-to-line or unread-occupant span's annulus, each conical seat's shell (FZ1 to FZ8). Clearance spans, grips and continuations declare none: a shaft that touches a clearance hole is not verified.
- **The original is the control** (FT9): checked once per run, at the first interface pass, at its own occurrences with the same zones; if it is not verified, no candidate's fit is `verified` (a `Reject` stays a `Reject`). P2: every corpus original whose interface passes is verified (56 of 57) but s20's, whose assembly puts a block 0.05 mm into the plate by construction. Of the fixture originals whose interface passes, AS1's ac and ug (8) are verified; AS1-md's 4 are `uncertainty_above_tolerance`, since md states 0.01 mm for its root placement, exactly t (section 5.3, open question 1).
- **The prototype works on the corpus.** P2 at fitment's own placements: the 3 `collides` cases interfere (299.4, 56.5 and 4 × 5.97 mm³), the 28 `fits` cases that pass their interface are verified, in 0.00 to 0.31 s each, and 26 of them keep it once free placements are applied (P6); of the 41 `does_not_fit` cases fitment already rejects at a placement, 27 also interfere, 2 raise a kernel warning and 12 are verified (their defects keep the body clear). Runs in order, in reverse order and in a fresh process per case are identical to the last digit (`p2_determinism.txt`).
- **Rules in Rust, geometry in Python** (M7-9, M7-36). fitment-core lists the bodies, their transforms, landmark faces, each pair's tolerances and the zones from its own reading; the helper builds solids, runs booleans and distances, and returns measures; the trait carries measures, and only core's `fit::decide` makes a status (FV1 to FV9). Every measure core can check, it checks: placed landmarks and boxes, zones' volumes, centroids and boxes, conservation across every cut, and a one-to-one map between its bodies and every solid the kernel transferred (FB3, FB6, FZ7, FZ9, FY4). Which pairs are tested is decided on the kernel's own boxes (FT8).
- **The status.** Interference of at least 1e-6 mm³ outside the zones: `interferes`, a `Reject`. Any kernel warning or helper exception, an invalid body, a shape tolerance above t/10, an uncertainty at or above t, a placement not unique, a binding or cross-check that disagrees, an unclaimed or untested body, a proximity, non-analytic geometry within reach, an interference below 1e-6 mm³, a control that fails: `not_verified`, an `InterfaceMatch`. Only everything clear is `verified` (FV2 to FV7). Open CASCADE's Python binding exposes no warnings on `BRepAlgoAPI_Common` in 8.0.1; the helper uses `BOPAlgo_BOP`, which does (P1, M7-17).
- **The helper's process.** One session per run (the CLI) or per job (the server), holding at most `--fit-jobs` helpers (min(jobs, 4)); each helper is long-lived, loads the assembly once and judges candidate after candidate, and is replaced after any failure or 256 requests, its scene re-validated (FL1 to FL3). uv runs only in `fitment fit setup`, which writes a manifest; runs and the server spawn the base interpreter the manifest names with the environment's site-packages added, never uv or a launcher, so a kill reaches the process that holds the kernel (FL2, M7-12). Frames are length-prefixed binary on a private file descriptor, one channel per process, each reply echoing its request's nonce and digest: Open CASCADE prints its errors on standard output (P3), so the helper moves fd 1 onto stderr before importing it (FH2).
- **A stale or wrong helper is refused** (FH3): the helper script and its lock are embedded in `fitment-fit`, written to the fit directory by setup, checked on disk before every spawn, and the helper's handshake must state protocol 1, its pid, the script's and the lock's SHA-256, the site it added, cadquery-ocp-novtk 8.0.1.1.0 and Open CASCADE 8.0.1, as the manifest says. A helper that cannot start (a missing shared library is named) refuses `--fit` (exit 1) or the server's start, never a silent `"none"`.
- **Failures are never a pass** (FV8). A timeout or a resource kill (`SIGXCPU`, `SIGXFSZ`, a `SIGKILL` fitment did not send) kills or reaps the helper; that candidate's fit is `not_verified` and the run is incomplete (exit 1, a `failed` job), since either depends on the machine. A crash (`SIGSEGV`, `SIGBUS`, `SIGABRT`, `SIGFPE`) is retried once in a fresh helper; a second is `not_verified` (`kernel_crashed`). Memory is bounded by `RLIMIT_AS` on Linux and a job object on Windows, CPU per check by `RLIMIT_CPU` and an alarm, and every check by its deadline.
- **The document** (M7-8). A run without a fit verifier writes `fitment.match` v1, byte for byte as 0.3.0 does; `MATCH_RULES_VERSION` stays 1. A run with one writes `fitment.match` **v2**, v1's superset: the verifier's identity, the fit's parameters, and per candidate and socket the fit's evidence; the kernel's measured numbers sit in `measured` objects outside the digest, so two OSes agree on the digest wherever they agree on every decision; each pair's tolerances, the freedom and the alternatives are in the digest (FM1 to FM7). v2 has its own output types, so v1's never change. The fit's own rules are versioned by `FIT_RULES_VERSION` 1.
- **Free placements** (M7-5): D1's rule stands. A placement the interface leaves free is `not_verified` (`placement_not_unique`), unless the only freedom is a turn about an axis the candidate is axisymmetric about, from fitment's own topology. Freedom is found by perturbing the chosen placement and re-checking the interface, whatever seed found it (FT10, FC4). Measured (P6): 7 of the 33 socket results of the interface-passing cases have a free turn; 5 are axisymmetric and excused; 2 `fits` cases of 28 lose their `Match`. A swept envelope over the turn is deferred (M7-52).
- **Packaging.** `fitment-fit`, a published, pure-Rust, MIT library holding the protocol, the session and the embedded script, is the only crate that spawns a process. The CLI's `fit` feature, on by default, adds `fitment match --fit ASM`, `--fit-jobs`, `--max-fits` and `fitment fit setup`; no Open CASCADE is in any crate, binary or official image (FG1 to FG6). The official image is unchanged and refuses `fit.verifier = "occt-helper"` at start; a committed recipe, `deploy/Containerfile.fit`, builds a user's image with the locked environment and manifest baked in and the Debian libraries OCP needs, runs read-only as user 65532, and CI builds and smoke-tests it without ever pushing or uploading it (M7-20, FX8).
- **The catalog** reads a blob only for a candidate whose every socket interface passed, its SHA-256 checked (QR13, ST11); with a verifier, dedup shares nothing, as M5 built it (FC6). A run's fits are capped deterministically (`limits.max_fits`, `--max-fits`, `too_large`).
- **Windows** (M7-50): a PR-gating three-OS `fit-kernel` job from PR 2b, the full Windows corpus weekly, and no `verified` on Windows until that weekly run is green.
- **D7's grip as a warning is deferred** past M7 (M7-23): it needs neighbours moved along their axes to reseat, a kinematic model of fastener stacks that the fit check does not need, and it would recover about four corpus cases (section 12).
- **Ten PRs** (section 11): 1a the pure fit module; 1b the matcher, the v2 writer and its schema; 2a process and protocol with a fake helper on three OSes; 2b the real helper and the `fit-kernel` job; 3 the CLI and the catalog; 4 the corpus, the oracle and the fit job; 5a the server; 5b the image recipe; 6 the exit; 7 the release 0.4.0.

## 4. Rules

### 4.1 The check (FT)

- **FT1. What a fit claims.** Static interference and clearance of the candidate's placed body against the modelled solids of this assembly, within each pair's tolerances (FT7), at the placements the evidence states, which are unique or free only in a turn the candidate's shape does not see (FT10). Nothing about motion, tolerance stack-up, assembly order or tool access, unmodelled items (fasteners not in the file, adhesives, cables), threads, or the fasteners a new part might need; every fit's evidence lists these as not checked (FT12).
- **FT2. Placements.** At each occurrence k of each socket, the candidate is placed by T_k · A, where A is the placement the interface passed at (M4's AL1, `SocketResult.placement`) and T_k the occurrence's transform from the socket document. Never re-aligned: the fit is a check of the interface's placement, not a search for another. An occurrence the matcher could not place gets no fit (M4's existing rule: "an occurrence of the socket could not be placed").
- **FT3. The check set.** For occurrence k: every solid of every leaf occurrence of the assembly but the selected occurrences of the target; the unselected occurrences of the target (under `--occurrence`) as ordinary neighbours; and the candidate's own placed copy at every other selected occurrence, in every socket (M3's V1). Neighbour-to-neighbour pairs are never tested. Each copy pair is tested once for each of its two occurrences, so each side's zones apply.
- **FT4. Interference.** For each pair (C_k, B) within reach (FT8): V = Common(C_k, B) through `BOPAlgo_BOP`, no fuzzy value, serial, non-destructive (FH4); R = V less every zone of (k, B) at depth ε_pair (FT7, FZ6). R's volume is the interference. Common catches containment as well as crossing (P1: a 2 mm box inside a 10 mm box gives 8 mm³). Outside the zones the depth of an overlap does not matter: any R of 1e-6 mm³ or more is an interference, however shallow (1e-6 mm over 1 mm² is 1e-6 mm³); an overlap Open CASCADE absorbs (1e-7 mm and less, P1) is FT5's.
- **FT5. Proximity.** For each pair within reach whose distance (BRepExtrema, its inner solution read as 0) is at most the pair's threshold t + u_pair (FT7): the distance from C_k to B less every zone of (k, B) at depth ε_pair + t + u_pair. If that is at most t + u_pair, the pair is in proximity. The zones' extra depth keeps their own cut faces beyond the threshold from any face a declared contact holds, so a contact inside a zone never reads as one outside it (P2: no original of the corpus with a passing interface reads a proximity). BRepExtrema's minimum is exact for analytic face pairs; on a B-spline or other non-analytic face its extrema search can settle on a local minimum, so a pair with such a face on either body within reach is `not_verified`, `unsupported_geometry`, rather than trusted (FV4).
- **FT6. Inputs fit to be judged.** The candidate and every body of a checked pair are valid (`BRepCheck_Analyzer`), with positive volume, and the largest sub-shape tolerance of each (`ShapeAnalysis_ShapeTolerance`) is at most t/10: Open CASCADE absorbs overlaps up to a shape's tolerance (P7 (a): a body at tolerance 1e-4 mm absorbs an overlap of 5e-5 mm, one at 1e-3 mm absorbs 5e-4 mm and raises an alert at 2e-3 mm). P4: the fixtures' solids are at 1e-7 or 1e-5 mm but three: two of vaccase's (up to 0.0029 mm) and one of the weldment's (0.058 mm).
- **FT7. Each pair's own tolerances.** For a pair (C_k, B), u_pair is the largest of: the candidate's `max_uncertainty_mm` (the original's own for the control), the occurrence's stated `part_uncertainty_mm` and `placement_uncertainty_mm`, B's own stated ones (every leaf's, from core's scene, FB1; a copy's are the candidate's and its occurrence's), and `GUARD_MM` (1e-6 mm). With t the policy's `linear_mm`:
  - **ε_pair = t − u_pair** is the depth of every interference zone (FZ6). If ε_pair ≤ 0 the pair is `not_verified`, `uncertainty_above_tolerance`: the files do not locate the two bodies to within the tolerance, so no contact between them can be told from an overlap;
  - **the proximity threshold is t + u_pair**: a gap at or under it is a proximity (Soundness rule 4: the edge rejects; P2 reads the four `bracket_dia_clear` frames, clearances at exactly c + 2τ, at 0.009999999993 to 0.00999999999999 mm, and `tube50_tau_edge_skew` at 0.0100000000006 mm, all proximities);
  - **proximity zones are cut at ε_pair + t + u_pair** (FT5).
  The three values are in each pair's evidence and in the digest (FM3, FM5). As first designed, δ = max(t, u) also set the zones' depth, so an uncertain neighbour deepened its own zones: P7 (b) has a boss 0.04 mm into a cited plate face verified at u = 0.058 mm (the weldment's stated uncertainty); as revised it is `interferes` at u up to 0.0099 mm and `uncertainty_above_tolerance` from u = t on.
- **FT8. Reach, from both readers' boxes.** Which pairs are tested is decided on the hull of Open CASCADE's box and fitment's own, never on either alone: the `scene` reply gives every body's box, a `place` request (FH4) returns the placed candidate's box at every placement, and each body's box for reach is the hull of the kernel's and of its part's sound box (`Part.extent`, which holds every point) moved where fitment places the body; core lists the pairs whose boxes, each enlarged by ε_pair + 2(t + u_pair) plus its largest shape tolerance plus 1e-6 mm, overlap. A kernel box too small (a helper's bug) so culls nothing, and fitment's box alone never culls a body the kernel places nearer. A body whose box either reader cannot give (no sound box of fitment's, the kernel's unsound: a NaN, OCCT's empty box; a tolerance not a finite non-negative number) is within reach of everything, never culled. (The review of PR 1a: the first build culled on the kernel's box alone.) Boxes are Open CASCADE's ordinary (non-optimal) ones: D1 found the optimal box too small on one B-spline case (OCCT #1604). The `check` reply then states, for every body of the scene and every placement, whether the helper's own boxes overlap within that reach; a body the helper finds within reach that is not in the request's pairs makes the fit `not_verified`, `untested_body`. A body outside reach is clear and is counted, not listed.
- **FT9. The control.** The original is checked as a candidate would be: its own solid at each selected occurrence (T_k, the identity placement), against the same check set, with the same zones and its own u_pair, its copies being the originals. One rule in the CLI and the server (M7-47): the control runs once per session, when the first candidate's interface passes, before that candidate's fit; a run in which no candidate passes runs none, and the document says so (`fit.control: null`). Its status is recorded once in the document (FM2). The control's placements are the assembly's own, given, never chosen, so FT10 does not apply to it. If it is not `verified`, every candidate's fit that would otherwise be `verified` is `not_verified`, reason `original_fails_fit` with the control's own code (FV5); a candidate that `interferes` still interferes.
- **FT10. Free placements.** D1's rule stands (M7-5): a placement the interface leaves free is not a placement the fit can vouch for. Freedom is derived from the placement itself, never from the seeding path that found it (FC4): the matcher moves the chosen placement A by each motion of a set built from the socket's own geometry (turns of 1°, 0.5° and 0.25° either way about every requirement axis and every seat normal; slides of 0.1, 0.05 and 0.02 mm either way along every such axis and normal and along two directions in every seat's plane) and checks the interface again at each (`verify_at`); a motion of which any size after which every requirement that passes at A still passes is free. (The review of PR 1a added the smaller sizes, so a turn free over less than 1° is found; it costs no `Match` on the corpus.) A free turn is excused when the candidate is axisymmetric about the turn's axis, from fitment's own topology of it in its own frame: every face a plane square to the axis, or a cylinder, cone or torus on it, and no `other` face. Any other free motion (a turn about an axis the candidate is not symmetric about, any slide) makes the fit `not_verified`, `placement_not_unique`, and no fit is asked. P6 on the corpus: 7 of the 33 socket results of the interface-passing cases have a free turn, all one-axis seeds; 5 of them axisymmetric (a washer, a bearing, a tube, a screw), excused; 2 not (`m_housing04`, `m_s21_block`), so 2 of the 28 `fits` cases lose their `Match`; none of the 3 `collides` cases and no slide is free. A swept envelope of the candidate over a free turn would recover those two; it is named and deferred (M7-52).
- **FT11. When.** The fit is asked once per candidate, after its interface passed at every socket, with every occurrence of every socket in one request. A candidate that rejects at any socket gets no fit; each socket that passed records `not_checked`, reason `another socket's interface rejected` (`INTERFACE_REJECTED_ELSEWHERE`), and each that failed `interface rejected` as today. A candidate whose every socket passed but one of whose placements is not unique gets no fit either (FV6). With `NoFitVerifier` nothing changes: every passing socket records `no fit verifier in this build`, so every 0.3.0 document is unchanged (VT52).
- **FT12. The scope stated.** Every fit's evidence carries the closed list `scope_not_checked`: `motion`, `tolerance_stack_up`, `assembly_order`, `tool_access`, `unmodelled_items`, `threads`, `new_fasteners`, and `alternative_placements` (FM3: the evidence lists every other placement the interface passed at, and the fit vouches for the stated one only). Soundness rule 5.

### 4.2 Zones (FZ)

A zone is a region where the socket declares that the target and a named body meet: there, a body may touch the candidate and overlap it by up to ε_pair, and nowhere else.

- **FZ1. From the socket, in core.** `fitment_core::fit::zones` derives every zone from the socket document's requirements, for each (occurrence, body) pair, as an analytic description (FZ2 to FZ4) the helper builds (FZ7). `unresolved` requirements declare none. A requirement removed in review keeps its zones: a removal waives what a candidate must satisfy, not the contact the assembly makes. Zones never come from the helper's own reading.
- **FZ2. A seat's slab.** For each contact of each `seat` and each entry of its `by` list (neighbour path, neighbour face `#id`): the neighbour's face, a plane, moved by −d along its normal and swept 2d along it (a prism), d being the zone's depth (FZ6). Only that neighbour, only at the occurrences the contact's sources name: one neighbour's slab never excuses another body (FK2's `f_other_zone`).
- **FZ3. A span's annulus.** For each span of a `bore` or `shaft` whose `fit` is `interference`, `line_to_line` or (pinned) `not_read`, and each source that names it (`span_mm` set) at this occurrence: the annulus about the requirement's axis, placed by T_k, from station `from_mm − d` to `to_mm + d`, between the smaller radius less d and the larger plus d, the radii being the span's own (hole or shaft) and the neighbour's: own − margin in a bore, own + margin about a shaft (the source's `margin_mm`, 0 when null). Only the source's neighbour. P2 found both needs: the bolt in s01's nut overlaps by a 0.5 mm radial margin (the annulus from 2.5 − d to 3.0 + d), and s48's bushing holds a D-shaft its rules do not read (`not_read`, pinned: the wall's own radius ± d).
- **FZ4. A conical seat's shell.** For each `conical_seat` with a radial range: the cone surface over `radii_mm` widened by d each way, swept d either side of the surface along its normal, opening from the apex towards the axis's point; for each source's neighbour at this occurrence. Without it, P2 read every countersunk screw against its plate as a proximity (s18, s58, s60).
- **FZ5. Copies.** A zone whose neighbour is another selected occurrence (a source flagged `self`) applies to the pair of copies at those two occurrences; its face is the original's at that occurrence, which the candidate's face is within ε of by the interface.
- **FZ6. Depths.** For a pair, d = ε_pair = t − u_pair for interference (FT4), and ε_pair + t + u_pair for proximity (FT5); a pair with ε_pair ≤ 0 has no zone and is `uncertainty_above_tolerance` (FT7). An overlap deeper than ε_pair inside a slab is interference: a seat face 0.015 mm proud is not a contact.
- **FZ7. Built from primitives, and checked.** The helper builds a slab with `BRepPrimAPI_MakePrism` of the cited face and annuli and shells with `BRepPrimAPI_MakeRevol` of a quadrilateral, and returns each zone's volume, centroid and box; core compares each with its closed form (a prism's area × 2d, its face's centroid, its face's box swept; a revolved quadrilateral by Pappus, its centroid on the axis) within 1e-9 relative and 1e-6 mm. A volume alone cannot see a zone placed at the wrong depth (P7 (c): a 2 mm slab has 200 mm³ at the face and 5 mm under it), hence the centroid and box. A zone whose face has no closed form in fitment's reading (a cited face with an unknown outline, or not a plane where a slab needs one) is not built: the pair is `not_verified`, `zone_unbuilt`. A zone that disagrees is `zone_unbuilt` too. P1: `BRepOffsetAPI_MakeOffsetShape` thickening a face reports done with an empty solid for a plane and a wrong one for a cylinder (189.75 mm³ for 1.88), so no kernel offset builds a zone.
- **FZ8. Nothing else is declared.** Clearance spans, grips, continuations (J rules), shaft ends past their spans (`max_extra_mm`, M4-11) and left-out holes declare no zone: a shaft that touches the wall of its clearance hole, or a boss that reaches a floor, is a proximity or an interference.
- **FZ9. Each zone's neighbour is where it should be.** For each zone used at an occurrence, the body it excuses must come within t + u_pair of the placed candidate (the contact the zone declares exists at this placement); a zone whose body is farther is `binding_failed`, since the candidate and the zone cannot both be where core put them.

### 4.3 Binding: the helper's scene is fitment's (FB)

- **FB1. Core lists the bodies.** `FitScene::of(assembly_bytes, &SocketDocument)` reads the assembly with fitment's own reader (the same `read_assembly` the socket came from; its SHA-256 must be the socket's `source`, else `FitSceneError::NotTheSource`) and lists every leaf occurrence: its path, its definition, its solid's `#id` and its faces' `#id`s, its transform to the root, its stated uncertainties, and whether it is selected. Bodies are keyed by occurrence path. A refused or changed assembly is no scene: the run is refused before any candidate (FU2).
- **FB2. The helper finds each body by its faces and its placement.** It reads the assembly once through XCAF (`STEPCAFControl_Reader`, as the oracle does), binds every face of every leaf to its `#id` through the transfer reader (`EntityFromShapeResult`, the oracle's `face_id`), and identifies each leaf by the set of its faces' `#id`s and its composed location. Core sends each body's face `#id`s (from fitment's own topology of that solid) and transform; the helper must find exactly one leaf with those faces whose location agrees with fitment's transform within 1e-9 in rotation and 1e-6 mm in translation, else `binding_failed`. P5: on every corpus and fixture assembly probed (13 files, 265 leaves, `moon_buggy_asm` and the weldment included) every face binds and the (faces, transform) keys are all distinct; a solid's own `#id` finds nothing, since `ShapeResult` holds results for the roots only; and XCAF's transforms alone do not identify a leaf (P2: `moon_buggy_asm` has two leaves at one transform).
- **FB3. Landmarks and boxes.** For each body and for the candidate, core names landmark faces in a fixed order (the two largest planes by known area, then the two largest full cylinders, then, while their normals and axes span fewer than three directions or leave a slide that moves none of them, each further plane and full cylinder that adds one: a block whose largest faces are all square to one axis gets its sides too, so an in-plane slide of a mapped body moves a landmark) with fitment's own values in the root frame: a plane's unit normal and offset, a cylinder's axis and radius. The helper reads each face by its `#id`, places it, and returns its values, the candidate's at every placement (from `place`, FH4); core requires agreement within 1e-6 mm and 1e-9 in direction. Every kernel box, the placed candidate's at each placement and each body's, must be sound and lie within fitment's own extent of the part moved by the same placement, widened by its shape tolerance and 1e-6 mm (a box moved off the axes is larger than the body's own, so the kernel's need not hold it); a box too small is FT8's, which never culls on the kernel's box alone. A disagreement is `not_verified` (`binding_failed`). This catches a unit, a frame, a mapped item or a placement read one way by stepq and another by Open CASCADE.
- **FB4. The candidate.** A file candidate's bytes are the ones M2's gate accepted; the helper hashes what it received and reports the SHA-256, which core compares with the candidate's `source.sha256`. It must hold one solid. A component candidate is named by its assembly's SHA-256, its occurrence and its faces' `#id`s, and that assembly is loaded as a scene of its own when it is not the socket's.
- **FB5. One assembly, one scene.** A helper holds at most two scenes (the socket's and one component source), keyed by SHA-256; a scene request for a third replaces the older component scene.
- **FB6. The scene is complete.** The `scene` reply lists every solid Open CASCADE transferred from the assembly (its faces' `#id`s, its location, its box), not only those core asked for. Core requires a one-to-one map between its bodies and those solids: a body of core's that the helper did not find, or found twice, is `binding_failed`; a solid the helper transferred that no body of core's claims is `unclaimed_body` when its box is within reach of any placement (it could be material the check never tested, which M3's section 4.4 promised would never pass silently), and is counted otherwise.

### 4.4 The fit's status from measures (FV)

- **FV1. Decided in core.** `fitment_core::fit::decide(&FitPlan, &FitMeasures) -> FitOutcome` turns the helper's measures into the per-socket and per-candidate `FitStatus` and its evidence. It is pure, and every rule below is a branch of it. The helper returns numbers, alert keys and booleans, never a status.
- **FV2. Alerts.** Any warning or error a boolean or a distance reports (`BOPAlgo_Options::HasWarnings`, `HasErrors`, `BRepExtrema_DistShapeShape::IsDone` false), and any Python exception or null result the helper meets while measuring a pair, a zone or the control (reported per pair as the alert `helper_exception`, the request carrying on with the next pair): `not_verified`, code `kernel_alert`, with the alert's class name (`BOPAlgo_AlertFaceBuilderUnusedEdges`, `helper_exception`) in the evidence. A kernel test injects an exception into one pair (VT71).
- **FV3. Interference.** A residual R ≥ `FIT_VOLUME_MM3` (1e-6 mm³) outside the zones, at any depth (FT4): `interferes`, with the pair, R, V and R's box. 0 < R < 1e-6 mm³: `not_verified`, `below_resolution`. P2: every residual of every passing original and `fits` case is exactly 0; the smallest interference P2 measures is 0.31 mm³ (`m_plate16_635`, a `fits_functional` case its interface rejects under strict); the band below 1e-6 mm³ is met by `m_plate03_pin_shift_tol` (an `edge` case, 4.5e-7 mm³).
- **FV4. Proximity and unsupported geometry.** A pair in proximity (FT5): `not_verified`, `proximity`, with the pair and the distance; never `interferes`, since a body within the threshold may only touch. A pair with a non-analytic face within reach: `not_verified`, `unsupported_geometry` (FT5).
- **FV5. The control.** A control not `verified`: every candidate's fit that would otherwise be `verified` is `not_verified`, `original_fails_fit`, the control's own code beside it. A candidate that `interferes` keeps `interferes`: a `Reject` stays a `Reject`, since its interference is outside every zone whatever the original does.
- **FV6. Placements not unique.** A placement with a free motion FT10 does not excuse: `not_verified`, `placement_not_unique`, the free motions listed; no fit is asked for that candidate.
- **FV7. Aggregation.** Per socket: `interferes` if any of its occurrences' pairs interferes (FV3); else `not_verified` if any rule of FV2 to FV6 or FV8 applies to it; else `verified`, with the kernel and version. Per candidate: M4's VD4, unchanged (`interferes` over `not_verified` over `verified`; the kernel and version must agree). A copy pair's interference counts for both occurrences' sockets.
- **FV8. Process failures.** fitment-fit classifies how a helper ended:
  - **a timeout** (FL4), **a resource limit** (`SIGXCPU`, `SIGXFSZ`, or a `SIGKILL` fitment did not send, the mark of an out-of-memory killer) **or a cancel**: the helper killed or reaped, that candidate's fit `not_verified` with `helper_timeout`, `resource_exhausted` or `cancelled`, and the run marked incomplete: the CLI exits 1 after writing the document, the server's job ends `failed` (a cancel: `cancelled`, no document), its document `complete: false` (M6's rule for a run the CLI ends with exit 1). A timeout or a resource kill depends on the machine, so a document that holds one never claims to be complete;
  - **a crash** (`SIGSEGV`, `SIGBUS`, `SIGABRT`, `SIGFPE`; on Windows an exit code from the `0xC0000000` range): the request is retried once in a fresh helper; a second crash is `not_verified`, `kernel_crashed`, and the run stays complete, since the same file crashes the same kernel;
  - **a malformed frame, a reply that breaks the protocol, or a write that misses its deadline** (FL4): `not_verified`, `helper_protocol`, the helper replaced, the run incomplete.
  None is ever `verified`.
- **FV9. Closed reasons.** Every `not_verified` and `not_checked` reason is a code from a closed list, with a fixed sentence built from values: `uncertainty_above_tolerance`, `placement_not_unique`, `kernel_alert`, `below_resolution`, `proximity`, `unsupported_geometry`, `original_fails_fit`, `invalid_body`, `tolerance_too_large`, `binding_failed`, `unclaimed_body`, `untested_body`, `zone_unbuilt`, `conservation`, `stored_bytes_unreadable`, `helper_timeout`, `resource_exhausted`, `cancelled`, `kernel_crashed`, `helper_protocol`, `helper_unavailable`. No path, host, time or helper stderr enters a document; stderr's last 64 KiB go to the log only, escaped.

### 4.5 The helper's protocol (FH)

- **FH1. Frames.** Each message in either direction is one frame: the four bytes `FFIT`, a 4-byte big-endian header length, the header (UTF-8 JSON, one object), then the payloads the header announces (`"payloads": [n₁, n₂, …]`, byte counts), each raw. No line structure, no base64. Caps, checked before allocation: a header at most 16 MiB, a payload at most 1 GiB (the server's uploads stop at 128 MiB, UP2), at most 4 payloads; a reply's header at most 16 MiB with no payload.
- **FH2. A private channel, one per process.** fitment-fit spawns the helper with stdin, stdout and stderr piped, and speaks to it on that one channel only: one request in flight, one helper per channel, no helper shared by two threads. The helper's first statements, before any import of OCP, duplicate fd 1 to a new descriptor for frames, then make fd 1 a copy of fd 2, so anything the kernel's C++ writes to `std::cout` reaches stderr (P3: `**** ERR StepFile : Undefined Parsing …` on stdout for a truncated file). Frames are read from fd 0 and written to the private descriptor, both binary and unbuffered. Every request carries a fresh 128-bit `nonce` and every reply echoes it with the SHA-256 of the request's header; a reply whose nonce or digest is not its request's is a protocol error (FV8). fitment-fit drains stderr on its own thread into a ring of 64 KiB, for the log.
- **FH3. The handshake and the helper's identity.** The first frame is `hello` from fitment-fit (`protocol`, and the manifest's values, FL2). The helper answers with: `protocol` (1); `pid` (`os.getpid()`, which must be the `Child::id()` fitment-fit spawned, so a launcher in between is caught); `helper_sha256`, the SHA-256 of its own file's bytes as it reads them; `lock_sha256`; `python` (`3.13.x`); `binding` (`cadquery-ocp-novtk 8.0.1.1.0`, from `importlib.metadata`); `kernel` (`Open CASCADE 8.0.1`, from OCP's `__version__` `8.0.1.1` and the library's own version string); `site` (the site-packages directory it added); `platform` (its `sysconfig` platform tag, for the log only); and `limits` (the limits it set and whether each took, FY3). fitment-fit refuses a helper whose protocol, pid, script or lock hash, binding, kernel or site is not the manifest's and the embedded constants': `helper_unavailable`, with what differed. An `ImportError` while importing OCP is answered as a `hello` that names the missing shared library (Linux without `libGL.so.1`, `libX11.so.6` or `libexpat.so.1`, the feasibility review's finding): `helper_unavailable`, with that sentence.
- **FH4. Operations.** Four after `hello`, each request with its `nonce` (FH2):
  - **`scene`** (payload: the assembly bytes): `scene` (its SHA-256, which the helper checks against the payload), `bodies` (each: key, faces' `#id`s, transform as 3 × 4 rows, landmarks). Reply: per body found or not, valid, volume, largest shape tolerance, box, each landmark's values; and every solid the reader transferred, claimed or not, with its faces' `#id`s, location and box (FB2, FB3, FB6, FT6). The helper keeps the scene's solids until a `scene` replaces it (FB5).
  - **`place`** (payload: the candidate's bytes, or none for a component): `scene`, `candidate` (its SHA-256, or a component's scene, faces and occurrence), its landmarks, `placements` (each: occurrence key, transform). Reply: the candidate's validity, tolerance and SHA-256; per placement its box and its landmarks' placed values (FB3, FB4, FT8). The helper keeps the placed candidate for the `check` that follows.
  - **`check`**: `pairs` (each: occurrence, body key or copy occurrence, `u_pair_mm`, `epsilon_mm`, `threshold_mm`, zones at ε_pair, zones at ε_pair + threshold). Reply per pair: `common_mm3`, `residual_mm3`, `inside_mm3` (the Common's volume within the zones, FY4), `residual_box`, `distance_mm` (null beyond the threshold), `zoned_distance_mm` (null when not asked), `body_kept_mm3` and `body_inside_mm3` (for the zoned distance's cut, FY4), `non_analytic` (whether either body has a non-analytic face within reach, FT5), `alerts` (class names, sorted); per zone its built volume, centroid and box (FZ7, FZ9); and for every scene body and placement, whether the helper's own boxes are within reach (FT8). Every boolean is `BOPAlgo_BOP` with `SetNonDestructive(True)`, so a scene's solids are never modified by a check and serve the next one unchanged (re-run P1 and P2 with it: unchanged; P2 the same bytes in one helper, in reverse order and in a fresh process per case, `p2_determinism.txt`).
  - **`bye`**: the helper exits 0. Its end of stdin also ends it.
  The control (FT9) is a `place` and a `check` whose candidate is the original component.
- **FH5. Strict decoding.** Both sides decode with `deny_unknown_fields`; every number must be finite (no NaN, no infinity, no −0.0 kept); every list is in the order the request gave; a reply to the wrong operation, with a missing pair, an extra pair, or a key not asked for is a protocol error (FV8). The helper refuses a request it cannot parse with an `error` frame (code, sentence) and stays up; fitment-fit treats an `error` frame as `helper_protocol`.
- **FH6. Versions.** `FIT_PROTOCOL` (1) rises with any change to a frame or an operation; the helper script's SHA-256 changes with any edit, and the embedded constant with it, so a stale script on disk is never run (FL6). `FIT_RULES_VERSION` (1, in core) rises with any change that could change a fit's status or evidence: a zone, a depth, a threshold, a reason, a pair chosen; it is in the v2 document and the fit baseline (FK6). The helper's pins (cadquery-ocp-novtk, Python) and the oracle's must be equal; CI's grep (`tools/verify-occt.py`'s and the helper's `dependencies` lines) fails otherwise, as decision 11 planned.

### 4.6 The helper's lifetime (FL)

- **FL1. Sessions.** An `OcctHelper` is the checked helper, built once from the manifest (FL2): the CLI at the start of a run, the server at start. An `OcctSession` is built per run (the CLI) or per match job (the server) from a helper, the socket's assembly bytes and the socket document; it implements `FitVerifier`. It holds a pool of at most `fit_jobs` helper processes (`--fit-jobs`, default min(`--jobs`, 4); the server's job, min(its CPU permits, `fit.jobs_per_job`, 4)), each taken by one judging thread for one candidate and given back; a judging thread that finds none free waits. Which helper serves a candidate never changes a byte of its result: each request carries everything it needs, the scene is the same bytes in every helper, and booleans never modify their arguments (FH4).
- **FL2. Set up once, then no uv.** `fitment fit setup` is the only step that runs uv, and the only one that may download: it writes the embedded script and lock into `fit.dir` (FL6), runs `uv run --locked --script fit_helper.py --print-env`, and writes `fit.dir/manifest.json`: the base interpreter (`sys._base_executable`), the environment's site-packages directory, the Python version, the binding's version, the script's and the lock's SHA-256, and the manifest's own format version. `fitment match --fit` and the server never run uv: they read the manifest, check the script, lock and interpreter files exist and the hashes are the embedded ones, and spawn `<base interpreter> -I <fit.dir>/fit_helper.py --site <site-packages>` on every OS; the helper adds the directory with `site.addsitedir` before importing OCP. No launcher sits between fitment and the kernel's process (no uv, and no Windows `python.exe` launcher in a venv's `Scripts`), so `Child::kill` ends the process holding Open CASCADE and `hello`'s `pid` is `Child::id()` (FH3). No manifest, or one that does not match: `helper_unavailable`, "run `fitment fit setup`". The image recipe bakes the manifest in at build time (FX8). P3: warm, 0.31 s to Python with OCP imported; the first setup downloads Python 3.13 and about 60 MB of wheels, and the first import after it took 6.3 s on a cold file cache.
- **FL3. Reuse and replacement.** A helper serves requests until it fails (any of FV8's cases), until it has served 256 checks, or until the session ends; a failed or retired helper is killed and reaped. A respawned helper loads the scene again and core re-validates it (FB2, FB3, FB6, the same bodies, volumes, tolerances and boxes as the first helper's, bit for bit) before its first check; a scene that differs is `binding_failed` for that candidate and the session is ended with the run incomplete. A helper never serves two sessions.
- **FL4. Deadlines, both ways.** `hello` and `scene` have `fit.setup_timeout` (300 s); a `place` and `check` together have `fit.timeout_seconds` (120 s, `--fit-timeout`). Writing a request has the same deadline: fitment-fit writes on its own writer thread, and the waiting thread kills the child if the write has not finished in time (a helper that stops reading would otherwise block a write into a full pipe forever). A reader thread delivers frames over a channel; the judging thread waits with the deadline, and in the server also wakes every 100 ms to read the job's cancel flag (FX4). Inside the helper, each check also arms `signal.alarm(timeout + 5)` and raises its soft `RLIMIT_CPU` to the CPU it has used plus the timeout, where the OS allows (FY3), so a helper whose parent cannot kill it still ends. On expiry or cancel: kill, reap, and FV8.
- **FL5. Ends.** At a session's end every helper gets `bye`, then 2 s, then a kill, and is reaped; a session dropped by a panic kills its helpers in `Drop`. A helper exits on its stdin's end of file, so fitment's own death ends it (the pipe closes). On Linux it also sets `PR_SET_PDEATHSIG` to `SIGKILL` through `ctypes`' `prctl` (which follows the thread that spawned it, so fitment-fit spawns every helper from a thread that lives as long as the checked helper, PR 2b). On Windows the helper joins a job object it creates through `ctypes`, which gives it its memory limit (FY3); the job's `JOB_OBJECT_LIMIT_KILL_ON_JOB_CLOSE` only ends processes the helper itself starts, since the helper holds the job's one handle, so on Windows only stdin's end of file ends a helper on fitment's death. (Corrected by the reviews of PR 2b.) Only in the server does the helper call `os.setsid`, so a terminal's signals never reach it; in the CLI it stays in fitment's process group, so a user's Ctrl-C ends both.
- **FL6. The fit directory.** `fit.dir` (`--fit-dir`, else `XDG_CACHE_HOME/fitment/fit`, `HOME/.cache/fitment/fit` or `LOCALAPPDATA/fitment/fit`; the server's `fit.dir`, required) holds `fit_helper.py`, `fit_helper.py.lock`, `manifest.json` and `tmp/`, the helpers' payload directory. It is created mode 0700 where the OS has modes and refused if it or a parent inside it is a symbolic link; `fitment fit setup` writes each file to a temporary name, syncs and renames it. Before every spawn the script and lock are read back and hashed; a file whose hash is not the embedded one is `helper_unavailable` ("run `fitment fit setup`"). `tmp/` (or a tmpfs mounted there) must be writable: each helper makes its own subdirectory, writes its payloads there for Open CASCADE's file reader, and removes it at exit; the server's start checks it is writable. uv's cache and Python installs stay where uv puts them at setup (`UV_CACHE_DIR`, `UV_PYTHON_INSTALL_DIR` passed through when set) and are only read afterwards.

### 4.7 Security (FY)

- **FY1. Only gated bytes reach the kernel.** The assembly is the socket's own (its SHA-256 the socket's `source`, which the socket gate accepted; FB1), and a file candidate is one M2's gate accepted (its `status` accepted, its SHA-256 sent and checked by the helper). Coordinates beyond 1e6 mm, unread units, several solids and dangling references are refused by fitment before Open CASCADE could misread them (P3: `1.E400` coordinates transfer in Open CASCADE without complaint; a dangling reference is read past).
- **FY2. One process, little authority.** The helper runs with an environment fitment-fit builds from nothing (only `SYSTEMROOT` on Windows), its working directory its own subdirectory of `fit.dir/tmp` (0700, removed at exit), no inherited descriptor but its three pipes (Rust's `Command` sets close-on-exec), `-I` (no user site, no environment variables, the script's directory not on the path), and, in the server, in a new session (`os.setsid`, FL5). It never needs the network: uv runs only at setup (FL2).
- **FY3. Resource bounds.** Before importing OCP the helper sets on itself, where the OS supports each, and reports in `hello` which took (`fitment fit setup` prints them):
  - `RLIMIT_AS` (`fit.memory_mib`, 4,096): enforced on Linux; macOS raises `ValueError` setting it, so it is reported as not set;
  - `RLIMIT_CPU`, soft, per check (FL4): the CPU used so far plus the check's timeout; the hard limit 256 checks' worth. Linux and macOS enforce it (`SIGXCPU`, a resource kill, FV8);
  - `RLIMIT_CORE` 0 (no core file of a user's geometry), `RLIMIT_NOFILE` 64, `RLIMIT_FSIZE` (the largest payload plus 1 MiB);
  - on Windows, a job object (FL5) with `JOB_OBJECT_LIMIT_PROCESS_MEMORY` at `fit.memory_mib` and kill-on-close.
  Where a bound is missing, the deadline (FL4) and, in the server, the container are the bounds. The memory a helper needs, measured (P3: 240 MiB after import, 315 MiB after a 6 MB file, 478 MiB after a 17.8 MB file of 400,000 entities), is estimated at 300 MiB plus 10 times the assembly's bytes plus 10 times the largest candidate's; that estimate is what the server reserves (FX3), and `RLIMIT_AS` stays a separate hard cap above it.
- **FY4. Strict replies, cross-checked.** FH5's decoding, FH1's caps; a reply's numbers outside their ranges (a negative volume, a residual above its common, a distance below zero) are a protocol error. Every measure core can check, it checks: the landmarks and boxes against fitment's reading (FB3); each zone's volume, centroid and box against its closed form (FZ7) and its neighbour's presence (FZ9); the candidate's SHA-256 (FB4); the scene's bijection (FB6); and **conservation** across every cut, vol(R) + vol(V within the zones) = vol(V), and vol(B less its zones) + vol(B within them) = vol(B), to 1e-9 relative, else `not_verified`, `conservation`. P2: conservation held to 1e-9 on every analytic pair of the corpus; on s11's B-spline bolts the quadrature misses it by 8.1e-5 (`p2_fit.txt`), so a B-spline pair is `conservation` as well as `unsupported_geometry`. A helper can make a fit `not_verified` or the run incomplete; no reply can make one `verified` but one in which every measure is clear and every cross-check holds.
- **FY5. The files on disk.** FL6's checks. The trust boundary is the user's account: a process of the same user can replace uv's environment or the fit directory, and then the manifest's checks, the handshake and the hashes catch a stale or edited script and a changed binding version, not a malicious one written to lie. The server runs as its own user, in its container, where nothing else writes.
- **FY6. In the server.** One session per job: a helper never holds two users' bytes (FX3). Helpers count against the job's CPU permits and its memory estimate (FX3), so the lanes bound them; a user's jobs are bounded by the per-owner caps and the job deadline; the fit's time is inside the deadline, never added to it. No response carries the helper's stderr, a path, or the platform tag; the log carries stderr escaped and capped (FV9).
- **FY7. What is not defended, and later.** Native code execution through a crafted file that passes the gates is contained only by the process, its limits (FY3), its parent-death signal and, in the server, its container: fitment-fit forbids `unsafe` and sets no seccomp or landlock policy. A stronger sandbox (bubblewrap, or seccomp and landlock applied by the helper on Linux) is named as later work (M7-51); M7 builds the cheap bounds: the per-check alarm and CPU limit, `PR_SET_PDEATHSIG`, and Windows' job object.

### 4.8 Changes to fitment-core (FC)

- **FC1. `FitRequest` v2, and measures across the trait.** Built only by core (it is `#[non_exhaustive]` already): `body` (`FitBody::Step { bytes, sha256 }`, `FitBody::Component { assembly_sha256, occurrence, faces }`, or `FitBody::Stored { sha256, fetch }` with a callback that returns the checked bytes, FC6), `socket_document`, `placements` (per socket and occurrence: T_k · A as `Rigid`), and `plan` (a `FitPlan` built by `fit::plan`: the bodies, landmarks, each pair's u_pair, ε_pair and threshold, and zones, FZ). The trait changes shape: `FitVerifier::measure(&FitRequest) -> Result<FitMeasures, FitFailure>` returns the helper's measures (or a process failure, FV8), and core alone calls `fit::decide` to make the `FitStatus` (FV1). `FitVerifier::identity` (new) gives the verifier's identity; `FitStatus::Verified` can be built only inside `fit::decide`, and only with that identity, so no verifier, fake or real, can hand core a `verified`. `NoFitVerifier` keeps its behaviour: no identity, no measures, every fit `not_checked` as in 0.3.0. The test-only `MeasuresVerifier` answers with given measures.
- **FC2. Once per candidate.** `Matcher::judge` judges every socket's interface first, then, if every socket passed and the verifier has an identity, works out each socket's freedom (FC4), and asks the verifier once for the candidate (FT11) unless a placement is not unique (FV6); it splits `fit::decide`'s outcome into each socket's `fit`. With no identity it keeps M4's per-socket call, so a `NoFitVerifier` run is unchanged to the byte (VT52).
- **FC3. `FitScene`, `fit::zones`, `fit::plan`, `fit::decide`, `fit::freedom`.** In `fit/`: the scene from the assembly bytes (FB1), the zones and their closed forms from a socket (FZ1 to FZ9), each pair's tolerances and the pairs from the scene and the kernel's boxes (FT3, FT7, FT8), the status from the measures (FV1 to FV9), and a placement's freedom (FC4). All pure; no I/O.
- **FC4. Freedom, from the placement.** `fit::freedom(&SocketView, &CandidateView, &Placement, &MatchPolicy) -> Freedom` builds FT10's motions from the socket's requirement axes and seat normals, applies each after the chosen placement at each of FT10's sizes, largest first, and re-checks the interface at it with the matcher's own `verify_at`; it returns the free motions and, for each free turn, whether the candidate is axisymmetric about its axis (FT10). It is independent of how alignment seeded the placement: AL5's axis pairs, AL8's one axis, AL9's cones (s18, s41, s58) and landmarks (s53) are all tested the same way. Cost: up to 12 motions per axis and 24 per seat normal, each one `verify_at`, the smaller sizes tried only where the larger is not free; P6's whole run over the corpus's 31 interface-passing candidates (33 socket results), reading each candidate included, took 1.1 s in release. `Placement` gains the result for the document (FM4).
- **FC5. Components.** `Matcher::judge_component` passes `FitBody::Component`; the original (`--original`, the control) is a component of the socket's own assembly.
- **FC6. Stored candidates.** `Matcher::judge_document_with_fetch(doc, source, fetch: &dyn Fn() -> Result<Vec<u8>, FetchError>)`: the closure is called only when the fit is asked (every socket's interface passed, a placement unique and a verifier with an identity); a fetch error is `not_verified`, `stored_bytes_unreadable`, and the run exits 1 (as a damaged row does). `fitment-catalog`'s `match_catalog` takes an optional `FitBlobs` (a read-only handle that reads one blob, hashes it against the row's SHA-256 and returns it, ST11) and passes a closure over it; `Snapshot` still has no field reaching `Blobs` (QR11). Dedup (QR6), as built in M5 and kept: with a verifier that has an identity there is no sharing at all, and every member is judged in full, interface and fit, with its own bytes. A two-phase core API (the interface shared, the fit per member) would save the interface's work on duplicates; it is not built, since duplicates in a catalog are rare and M5's code already takes the simple path.
- **FC7. The document's writer, with its own types.** v2 is written from its own output types (`matching/document_v2.rs`: `MatchDocumentV2`, `FitV2`, `PairV2`, …), built from the same results; v1's types and `FitStatus` are unchanged, so v1's bytes and schema stay frozen and `FitStatus` never grows a field. `MatchDocument::new` writes v1 when the verifier has no identity and v2 when it has one (FM1); `to_json` and the digest follow. No `from_json` (M4-23).
- **FC8. Versions.** `MATCH_RULES_VERSION` stays 1: nothing a run without a verifier does changes. `FIT_RULES_VERSION` 1 and `FIT_PROTOCOL` 1 are new; `MATCH_FORMAT_VERSION` stays 1 and `MATCH_FORMAT_VERSION_FIT` is 2.

### 4.9 The document (FM)

- **FM1. Two versions, chosen by the verifier.** With no fit verifier (`fit_verifier: null`), `fitment.match` v1 exactly as 0.3.0 writes it. With one, `format_version: 2`. v2 is v1 with the changes of FM2 to FM5 and nothing removed; v1's schema stays closed and its documents keep their bytes and digests.
- **FM2. The envelope.** `fit_verifier` becomes an object: `kernel` (`"open_cascade"`), `kernel_version` (`"8.0.1"`), `binding` (`"cadquery-ocp-novtk 8.0.1.1.0"`), `python` (`"3.13"`), `protocol` (1), `helper_sha256`, `lock_sha256`, `fit_rules_version` (1). A new `fit` object: `tolerance_mm` (t), `guard_mm`, `volume_resolution_mm3`, `tolerance_cap` (`"t/10"`), `max_fits` (the cap the run had, null for none), and `control`: per socket the original's fit, as a candidate's (FM3), or `null` when no candidate's interface passed (FT9).
- **FM3. A fit's evidence.** Every `fit` (per candidate and per socket) keeps v1's `status` and, by status: `verified`: `kernel`, `version`, `occurrences`, `bodies` (checked), `copies`, and `pairs`, each `{occurrence, body: {kind: neighbour | copy, path, product}, u_pair_mm, epsilon_mm, threshold_mm, zones: [{kind, requirement}], measured: {…}}`, plus `free_turns` (each excused turn: socket, axis, `axisymmetric: true`); `interferes`: `interferences`, each a pair as above with its `measured: {common_mm3, residual_mm3, box_mm}`; `not_verified`: `code` (FV9), `reason` (its sentence), `at` (the pairs, bodies or motions concerned, as above), `measured`; `not_checked`: `reason`, as v1. Every fit carries `scope_not_checked` (FT12). Each socket's result also lists `alternatives`: every other placement among those alignment tried at which every requirement passed, as transforms, for which no fit was asked.
- **FM4. Placements.** Each socket's `placement` gains `freedom`: the free motions FC4 found (each `{kind: turn | slide, about: [point, direction], axisymmetric}`), empty when the placement is unique.
- **FM5. The digest.** v2's digest is the SHA-256 of the compact document without `digest`, every `labels` and every `measured` object. The per-pair values core computes (`u_pair_mm`, `epsilon_mm`, `threshold_mm`, the zones), the codes, the pairs, the freedom and the alternatives are in it: they come from fitment's own reading and are the same on every OS. Kernel numbers vary in their last digits between platforms where every decision agrees; the digest covers the decisions, not those numbers. On one platform and helper the bytes are identical (P2, `p2_determinism.txt`).
- **FM6. Order and the summary.** MF5's order is unchanged (a `Match` first); the summary counts `match` for the first time. The table's header gains `fit: Open CASCADE 8.0.1 (helper 1a2b3c4d)` in place of `fit: not checked (no fit verifier in this build)`, and a candidate's line names its fit's status or code.
- **FM7. The schema.** The OpenAPI document gains v2's schema beside v1's (`https://fitment.dev/schemas/fitment.match/2` and `/1`), each closed (`additionalProperties: false`), generated from core's `schema` feature, and the served document stays the committed one (VN7's test); a match job's result names its document's version in `fitment.job` (an optional field within v1, M6-10).

### 4.10 Packaging (FG)

- **FG1. `fitment-fit`.** A library, published from 0.4.0 in lockstep with the others, MIT, `rust-version` 1.85, `unsafe_code = "forbid"`. Normal dependencies: `fitment-core`, `serde`, `serde_json`, `thiserror`, nothing else (SHA-256 is core's own). No Open CASCADE, no `cxx`, no binding of any kind (`deny.toml`'s bans stay). `tools/check-core.sh` gains it in its package loop (its packaged file list: `src/**`, `helper/**`, `README.md`, `LICENSE`) and checks its normal graph; `deny/sync.toml`'s check runs on it too, so tokio, hyper, axum and tower never enter it. `include_bytes!` of a path outside the package would break `cargo package` (workspace layout, section 7), hence `helper/` inside it.
- **FG2. What is embedded.** `helper/fit_helper.py` (the helper, a uv script whose inline metadata pins `cadquery-ocp-novtk==8.0.1.1.0` and `requires-python = "==3.13.*"`) and `helper/fit_helper.py.lock` (`uv lock --script`, with every wheel's hash), each with its SHA-256 as a constant generated by `build.rs`-free means: a test (`embedded_hashes_are_the_files`) recomputes them, so a stale constant fails CI. Its text is MIT, fitment's own; it imports OCP at run time and contains none.
- **FG3. The CLI's `fit` feature.** `fit = ["dep:fitment-fit"]`, in `default` beside `catalog` (M7-19). Without it, `--fit` and `fitment fit` exit 2 and say the build has no fit verifier, as `catalog` does.
- **FG4. The server's `fit` feature.** `fitment-server` gains `fit = ["dep:fitment-fit"]`, enabled in its release build and the image's; `fit.verifier = "occt-helper"` without the feature is a configuration error at start.
- **FG5. No Open CASCADE in official artifacts.** The crates hold the script's text; uv fetches Python and the wheel on the user's machine at `fitment fit setup`; `--fit` and the server never download (FL2). The official image stays as M6 shipped it (no Python, uv or Open CASCADE; the `container` job checks it). Where uv has no wheel (musl, Windows on ARM, macOS before 11), `fitment fit setup` fails and says so, and with no manifest `--fit` exits 1 and the server refuses to start with `occt-helper`. On Windows the helper answers `helper_unavailable` until the weekly Windows corpus is green (M7-50).
- **FG6. The release.** 0.4.0 in its own PR after M7's exit (M7-29), from that PR's commit: `fitment-core`, `fitment-catalog`, `fitment-fit`, then `fitment`; the image `ghcr.io/jchultarsky/fitment-server:0.4.0` by `image.yml` as for 0.3.0. cargo-semver-checks joins for `fitment-fit` after its first publish.

### 4.11 The CLI (FU)

```text
fitment match <SOCKET> (<CANDIDATE>... | --catalog DIR …) [--original ASM]
              [--fit <ASSEMBLY>] [--fit-jobs <N>] [--fit-timeout <SECONDS>] [--max-fits <N>]
              [--fit-dir <DIR>]
              …M4's and M5's options
fitment fit setup [--fit-dir <DIR>] [--uv <PATH>]
```

- **FU1. `--fit ASSEMBLY`.** Turns the fit verifier on, with the socket's own assembly (its SHA-256 must be the socket document's `source`, else exit 2 before any candidate, as `--original` is checked). `--require-fit` keeps its meaning: an `InterfaceMatch` is a failure (exit 5).
- **FU2. Before any candidate.** With `--fit`: read and check the manifest (FL2), check a helper (FH3), and build the scene (FB1). A helper that cannot be checked, or a scene that cannot be built: exit 1, with the reason (no manifest: run `fitment fit setup`; a hash or version that differs; a missing shared library; the assembly refused), nothing on stdout. The control runs when the first candidate's interface passes (FT9); one that fails is not an error: the run goes on, and stderr says once that the original fails its own fit check and why.
- **FU3. During the run.** Each candidate whose interface passes and whose placement is unique is fit-checked by one of the session's helpers (`--fit-jobs` many, default min(`--jobs`, 4)); stderr's summary line names the fit: `fit: Open CASCADE 8.0.1` and the counts by status. A timeout or a resource kill makes the run exit 1 after the document is written (FV8). With `--max-fits N`, a run in which more than N candidates pass their interface asks no fit and is refused before its document, exit 3, `too_large` (as M6's JS12 refuses an oversized exhaustive run): deterministic, since the count is the interface's.
- **FU4. `fitment fit setup`.** The only command that runs uv (`--uv`, default `uv` on `PATH`) and may download: writes the script and lock, resolves the environment, writes the manifest (FL2), then spawns a helper from the manifest exactly as a run would, runs `hello` and a built-in self-test (two unit boxes touching, overlapping by 1e-3 mm and apart by 0.02 mm, each with its expected Common and distance), prints the identity, the limits that took on this OS and the paths, and exits 0, or 1 with the reason (a missing shared library named, FH3). CI, the image recipe and a user's first use run it.
- **FU5. Exit codes.** VD14's, with `Match` now reachable: 0 at least one `Match`; 4 an `InterfaceMatch` at best; 5 every candidate rejected; 1 an error (now also: no manifest or a helper that cannot be checked, a fit timeout or resource kill, a helper protocol failure, a stored blob unreadable); 2 usage (also `--fit` without the feature, or naming another assembly); 3 refused input (also `--max-fits` exceeded).
- **FU6. A catalog run.** `fitment match S --catalog DIR --fit ASM`: the fit reads each passing candidate's blob through the catalog (FC6); stderr counts the blobs read.

### 4.12 The server (FX)

- **FX1. Configuration.** `[fit] verifier = "none" | "occt-helper"`, and for `occt-helper`: `dir` (the fit directory with its manifest, required; its `tmp/` writable, or a tmpfs mounted there), `timeout_seconds` (120), `setup_timeout_seconds` (300), `memory_mib` (4,096), `jobs_per_job` (4); and `limits.max_fits` (1,000): a run with more interface passes is refused `too_large` (FU3). The server never runs uv: `dir` must hold a manifest `fitment fit setup` wrote (FL2). Unknown keys are refused (`deny_unknown_fields`, as every section).
- **FX2. The start.** With `occt-helper`, `fitment-server serve` reads the manifest, checks a helper (FH3) and runs `fitment fit setup`'s self-test through it before it binds its port; a failure refuses the start with the reason (no manifest, a missing shared library, a hash that differs), never falling back to `"none"` (OP9, M6's F28). `fitment.info`'s `fit_verifier` names the kernel and version (DC11) and gains `fit`, the v2 envelope's `fit_verifier` object, an optional field within v1 (M6-10).
- **FX3. Sessions in jobs.** A match job with a verifier opens an `OcctSession` in its blocking thread (JS6) with the socket line's assembly bytes from the object store (OB3: read once, checked against its name), and holds at most min(its CPU permits, `jobs_per_job`) helpers. JS3's memory estimate gains, per helper, FY3's measured estimate (300 MiB plus 10 times the assembly's bytes plus 10 times the largest candidate's); `RLIMIT_AS` stays a separate cap. Helpers are spawned lazily, at the first candidate whose interface passes, and all killed when the job ends, however it ends. Each helper costs fitment-fit two threads that only block on its pipes (frames, stderr) and one writer thread per request in flight; they do no compute, so they sit outside the CPU budget, and SV7's ban on `std::thread::spawn` stays true of the server's own code, as it is for the catalog's scoped threads. `fitment_fit_helpers` (a gauge), `fitment_fit_checks_total{status}` and `fitment_fit_seconds` join the metrics (OP6).
- **FX4. Stopping.** JS8 gains a stop point: inside a fit, the waiting thread reads the job's flag every 100 ms and kills its helper; the candidate is not judged and the job ends as its stop says, with no document. So a fit never needs JS8's stuck-call watchdog; the watchdog stays for a core call.
- **FX5. Bytes for components and the catalog; a request's `fit`.** `POST /v1/matches` gains `fit: "auto" | "none"` (default `auto`): `auto` asks the configured verifier, `none` judges with `NoFitVerifier` and writes v1, as M6 did. With `auto` and a verifier, a run whose socket names no assembly the server holds (an imported socket line without its assembly) ends `refused`, exit 3, `fit_needs_assembly`, before anything is judged. A component candidate of another stored assembly: its bytes from the object store by SHA-256 (the job holds every assembly it names, OB4). A catalog run: the catalog's `FitBlobs` handle (FC6), opened read-only in the job's snapshot; a blob missing or damaged is `not_verified`, `stored_bytes_unreadable`, and the job ends `failed` with its document (`complete: false`), as the CLI exits 1.
- **FX6. Parity.** Under the same helper (the same script, lock and binding), every document a fit match job serves is the CLI's `--fit` bytes for the same inputs, and its exit code the CLI's; `fit_verifier` and `fit` included. A server built without `fit`, or configured `none`, serves v1 as in M6.
- **FX7. Faults.** XH12's fault tests gain the fit's: a helper crashing, hanging, answering garbage or a wrong protocol, killed mid-check by a cancel, a deadline or a drain, and the server killed during a fit, then restarted (SD4: a pure match job runs again once).
- **FX8. The image recipe.** `deploy/Containerfile.fit` builds `fitment-server-fit` (a local tag) from the official image of the same version:
  - a builder stage on `debian:bookworm-slim` copies uv from its pinned image (`ghcr.io/astral-sh/uv:VERSION`, by digest) and the two binaries from the official image, and runs `fitment fit setup --fit-dir /opt/fitment/fit` with `UV_CACHE_DIR` and `UV_PYTHON_INSTALL_DIR` under `/opt`, so Python, the locked environment and the manifest are baked in;
  - the final stage is `debian:bookworm-slim` with `libgl1`, `libx11-6` and `libexpat1` (OCP's Linux wheel loads `libGL.so.1`, `libX11.so.6` and `libexpat.so.1`; the feasibility review's probe found the import fails without them), their copyright files under `/usr/share/doc`, the Python install and environment from the builder, the two binaries, the official image's non-root user 65532, volumes and health check, `fit.verifier = "occt-helper"`, `fit.dir = /opt/fitment/fit` read-only, and its `tmp/` on a tmpfs (`--tmpfs /opt/fitment/fit/tmp`, documented, or a volume);
  - no uv in the final stage: the server never runs it (FL2).
  Its header says what it contains (Open CASCADE under LGPL-2.1 with its exception, the wheel's bundled libraries, the Debian libraries) and that whoever distributes the image owes their notices. CI's `container-fit` job builds it on every PR and runs `deploy/fit-smoke.sh` with `--read-only --user 65532 --tmpfs …` (the M6 smoke run, then s01's lug and bracket matched as `Reject` and `Match`); it pushes nothing, and uploads neither the image nor its layers as an artifact or to a cache; `image.yml` never builds it. That keeps it within D1 (open question 5, decided).
- **FX9. Exposure.** Only the match job's kinds spawn a helper; no endpoint reaches the helper, its directory or its stderr. A user's fit time is inside the job's deadline (JB7) and lane (JS1).

### 4.14 The oracle (FO)

- **FO1. Every `Match`, physically.** KO3 already checks every `InterfaceMatch` at fitment's placement; it now checks every `Match` the same way: no common volume with any neighbour or copy outside zones the oracle builds itself from the socket document at depth t (not from fitment's zones, and with its own code), by its own physics (`physics`, `regions`). Where FC4 excused a free turn, the oracle also checks the `Match` at 8 turns about that axis.
- **FO2. Every `interferes`, re-measured.** For each interference in a document, the oracle places the candidate and the named body as the document says and measures the Common less its own regions; it must exceed 1e-6 mm³ and agree with `residual_mm3` within 1e-6 relative. For `collides` cases, the collision is checked at the construction's placement, and at 8 turns where FK3 says a turn is free and the candidate axisymmetric.
- **FO3. Mutations.** KO6 gains three on v2 documents: an `interferes` turned `verified` (FO2 meets it), a `verified` on a `collides` case (FO1 meets it), and a `freedom` entry dropped from a placement (the scorer's checker meets it: VT76's counts no longer hold).

- **An uncertain neighbour (P7 (b), the soundness review's probe).** A boss 0.04 mm into a cited plate face: with δ = max(t, u) as first designed, `interferes` up to u = 0.01 mm and **`verified` at u = 0.058 mm**; as revised (FT7), `interferes` at u = 1e-7, 0.004 and 0.0099 mm (residual 0.03 to 0.0399 mm³) and `uncertainty_above_tolerance` at 0.01 and 0.058 mm.

### 5.4 The process (P3, P4, P5)

- `uv run --locked --script` of the oracle, warm: 0.35 s to exit; the environment's own interpreter with `-I`, importing OCP: 0.31 s warm, 6.3 s on its first run after the environment was made (a cold file cache).

- Binding (P5): a solid's own `#id` finds nothing after `TransferRoots`; faces bound by `#id` plus the composed location identify 265 of 265 leaves of 13 assemblies uniquely.
- The feasibility review's probes (its scratch, not committed here) found that OCP's Linux wheel needs `libGL.so.1`, `libX11.so.6` and `libexpat.so.1` on a bare Debian (FH3, FX8), that a venv's interpreter on Windows is a launcher (FL2), and that a uv-run helper's `--print-env` gives the environment's interpreter (FL2).

### 5.5 What was not measured

## 9. Risks

- R1. **The kernel's edge.** Open CASCADE absorbs overlaps of 1e-7 mm and less (P1), more for shapes of larger tolerance (P7 (a)). FT5's proximity at t + u_pair and FT6's cap of t/10 together keep any absorbed overlap inside a proximity, which is `not_verified`. A shape whose tolerance a file understates is outside this argument; FB3's landmarks catch gross disagreement only.
- R2. **Zones too generous.** A zone excuses up to ε_pair of overlap with one named body over one face or one span, at one occurrence, where that body is (FZ9). A defect that lives entirely inside a zone and is shallower than ε_pair is, by the policy, within the tolerance; a deeper one is interference (FZ6). FO1 builds its own zones at t, so it checks the fit's zones rather than sharing them.
- R3. **Placements.** A `Match` vouches for the stated placement, which is unique up to 1° and 0.1 mm (FT10) or free only in a turn the candidate's shape does not see. Alternative placements the interface also passes at are listed, not checked (FM3, FT12). A turn or slide free over less than the motions tried counts as fixed.
- R4. **False negatives from the fit.** Every `not_verified` costs a `Match`. The corpus measures: 2 of 28 `fits` cases (`placement_not_unique`); the fixtures with a passing interface, 4 of 12 (AS1-md, `uncertainty_above_tolerance`). Real assemblies with modelled thread overlaps no rule reads, cosmetic interferences, coarse stated uncertainties or B-spline contacts will cost more; the report lists every `not_verified` by code.
- R5. **Platforms.** Windows' kernel and process behaviour are unproven here; until the weekly Windows corpus run is green, Windows gives no `verified` (M7-50).
- R6. **uv.** uv runs only at setup (FL2); a uv upgrade that changes `--print-env`'s environment or the lock format fails setup loudly, never a run.
- R7. **Licences.** Unchanged from D1: fitment distributes no Open CASCADE; a user-built image does, and its builder owes the notices (FX8's header says so); issue #1564 is still open.
- R8. **Cost at catalog scale.** A socket that many catalog parts pass at the interface costs a fit each: at 1 s a fit, 1,000 interface passes take about 17 minutes on one helper, 4 minutes on four. `limits.max_fits` (1,000) and the job's deadline bound it deterministically (FU3, FX1); the weekly leg measures it (VT79).

## 12. What was rejected, and why

- **Differential fit against the original** (M7-1's alternative; the oracle's KO1): simpler and robust, but it accepts whatever the original does, including an assembly that already interferes, and it compares a candidate with the original, which PLAN rules out for the interface and this design keeps out of the fit.
- **δ = max(t, u) as the zones' depth** (the first design's FT7): an uncertain neighbour deepened its own zones (P7 (b)); C1's split replaces it.
- **A `Match` at any free turn** (the first design's M7-5, and the feasibility review's view): the soundness review's argument decides it. Fitment's claim is that the part fits where the interface puts it; where the interface does not put it anywhere in particular, a check at one turn says nothing of the turn the part ends up at, unless the part looks the same at every turn. The swept envelope of the candidate over the turn would recover `Match` there; it is deferred (M7-52).
- **Freedom from the seeding path** (the first design's FC4): it misses AL9's cones and landmarks; freedom comes from the placement.
- **Culling on fitment's extent:** the kernel decides where a body is (FT8).
- **Offsetting the candidate by δ** for the proximity test (D1's first sketch): the bracket offsets cleanly (P1), but an offset of a general solid can fail where the distance cannot, and the distance to the body less its zones answers the same question with one kernel call per pair.
- **Zones from thickened faces:** P1's `MakeOffsetShape` thickening gave an empty solid for a plane and a wrong one for a cylinder while saying done.
- **The helper deciding the status** (D1's sketch): the rules would be Python, untestable without uv, and a helper that lies would decide. Since the reviews, the trait carries measures and only core makes a status (FC1).
- **JSON lines on stdout** (D1, the workspace layout): the kernel writes on stdout (P3).
- **uv at run time** (the first design's FL2): a read-only image, an offline machine and a Windows venv launcher each break it; the manifest replaces it.
- **Bootstrapping the script over the pipe** (`python -I -c` reading the script from stdin, so nothing is written to disk; the feasibility review's L9, optional): it would remove FL6's files, but the manifest's checks need the files anyway; kept as a later option.
- **A process per request:** 0.3 s and an assembly read per candidate, with nothing gained over FL3's replacement after any failure.
- **A sidecar container for the server:** a network protocol, authentication and a kernel shared across users, for what a child process does.
- **A second official image with Open CASCADE:** D1 forbids it while #1564 is open.
- **D1's `Reject("undeclared contact")`** for a proximity (M7-4's alternative): a body that touches does not interfere (KC3).
- **A `fitment.match` v2 for every run:** every 0.3.0 document would change for no new content.
- **Squeezing the evidence into v1:** v1's schema is closed; the evidence would be strings.
- **A two-phase core API for dedup** (the interface shared, the fit per member): M5 built no sharing with a kernel named; kept (FC6).
- **D7's warning in M7** (M7-23). Building it means: finding the neighbours that bear on the gripped seats and every part that moves with them (a bolt, its nut, its washers, along one axis), moving each by the grip's difference, checking that the moved neighbours still meet their own mates, then running the fit in that moved scene; and a new format field whose meaning (a `Match` with a warning) needs its own review. It recovers the grip-only thickness changes (four corpus cases), and nothing in the fit check needs it. It is better done once real catalog parts show how often grips differ (D8, D9).
- **Monstertruck or a mesh check** as a source of `Match`: D1's reasons stand.
